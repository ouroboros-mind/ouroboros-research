## Question

After moving the stored mind to a second model, can a skill regrown from the mind's own verified practice, inside a presentation chosen before any skill is grown, beat the raw model on familiar task families without a registered loss on unseen families?

## Protocol (conceptual)

1. Calibrate the model and screen presentations before growing a skill.
2. Commit and publish a hash of the selection before the work it governs.
3. Harvest practice from the selected presentation and the raw presentation. Keep verified successes for training.
4. Build two nested training sets with the same example count, matched token counts and the same number of training steps. Adjust them only by swaps within the same task family and difficulty level.
5. Train and run the final test only if the training dose can be matched. A failed match stops the run with no decision before training.

## Results

Classified INCONCLUSIVE: stopped before training by its own pre-registered rule.

Calibration, the presentation screen and the practice harvest ran. A hash of the selection was committed and published; the author reports this preceded the work it governs. The selection record itself stays private because it names a protected method, so readers cannot check which presentation was used. This departs from the earlier note that it would be published with the result.

| Practice harvest | Episodes | Verified successes |
|---|---:|---:|
| Selected presentation | 1080 | 615 |
| Raw presentation | 1080 | 468 |

These are descriptive counts, not a registered test of the skill or its transfer.

The harvest yielded 86 usable examples per family against 160 planned. The two nested training sets had 258 examples each. Equal example counts did not give a matched training dose: one set carried about 20% fewer tokens. Examples from one task family were about 2.5 times shorter than the others.

After 52 swaps, the token gap was still 18%. No remaining swap within the same task family and difficulty level reduced it. The pre-registration says a failed match stops the run with no decision before training. The owner chose to honor that rule rather than change it after seeing the practice data.

Three amendments were made after the run began and before any training or final-test data. Two fixed checks in the early stages. The third fixed a check that miscounted training grants inherited from earlier experiments and made the training-set planner fast enough to finish.

The amendments are recorded in the private ledger and fixed by checkpoint 116. Unlike EXP-0050's amendments, they were not published as separate commitments before their governed work. The checkpoint does not establish that they were public before that work.

## What this means

The skill-regrowth question remains unanswered. No adapter was trained, so this run cannot show whether a regrown skill beats the raw model or avoids a loss on unseen families. The practice counts do not answer that question.

The stop followed the pre-registered rule. The training dose could not be matched with the allowed swaps, and the rule was kept after the practice data were seen.

## What changed next

The lesson is to match training dose on tokens, not example counts, and to dry-run the training-set planner on real practice data before the training window.
