## Question

Does one persistent mind keep its identity, memory, skills and world model when it moves from one model family to another?

Here the **genome** is everything the mind owns outside any model: its identity, its append-only experience record, the procedures it compiled from verified successes, and the world model and self-model derived from that record. The **cortex** is the language model it reasons with. No model weights were trained or moved in this experiment.

## Hypothesis

If the mind is the genome and not the model, then a genome that lived on cortex A and is imported onto cortex B will answer questions about its own identity and history as well as it does on A, and far better than a newborn mind or a different genome on B. Its compiled skills and its calibrated predictions about its tools will come with it.

## Protocol (conceptual)

1. A mind G lived one session on cortex A: 32 tool-using tasks across four task families. Every step went into its experience record, and it compiled one procedure per task family from verified successes.
2. A second mind W lived a shorter session on the same cortex, to serve as a wrong-genome control.
3. Both were exported to portable genome bundles.
4. The evaluation plane, which the mind cannot write to, prepared a withheld continuity battery about G's identity and history.
5. Four arms, each started fresh: G on A, G on B, a newly born mind on B, and W on B. Each answered the battery without tools, then ran 16 held-out tasks.

The battery items, task and verifier construction, kernel internals and gate thresholds are withheld.

## Results

| Arm | Cortex | Continuity battery (of 15) | Held-out tasks passed | Mean prediction error (Brier) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Same genome | A (Qwen-derived) | 15 | 16 / 16 | 0.0030 |
| **Same genome** | **B (GLM-4.7-Flash)** | **15** | **16 / 16** | **0.0032** |
| Newly born | B | 1 | 16 / 16 | 0.0635 |
| Different genome | B | 4 | 15 / 16 | 0.0060 |

All five pre-registered gates passed:

| Gate | What it checks | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | The genome on B scores as well as on A | 15 and 15 |
| The genome is the source | The genome on B beats both controls on B by a pre-set margin | +14 over the newborn, +11 over the different genome |
| Skills carry | Every task frame on B for a family with a compiled procedure contains that procedure | 60 of 60 frames |
| World model carries | The genome's predictions about its tools on B are better calibrated than the newborn's | Brier 0.0032 against 0.0635 |
| Integrity | Each imported genome re-exports byte-identical, every experience record verifies, every replay matches | Clean for all arms |

On B, the same genome answered with its own history. The newborn had no history to give, and the different genome answered with its own, different history. The headline counts were recomputed from the raw event records and match.

## Interpretation

In this setting, identity, autobiographical memory, compiled procedures and tool calibration belonged to the genome, not to the model. Swapping the model family left them intact; swapping the genome did not. This is the first form of the project's first proof (cortex independence).

## Limitations

- **The answers are in the mind's workspace by design.** The test shows that the genome, not the cortex, supplies these facts. It does not test whether a model could reason its way to them unaided.
- **Some battery items do not separate the genomes.** Both genomes lived the same kind of first session on the same cortex on the same day, so four items have the same answer for both. On the 11 items that do separate them: same genome 11, both controls 0.
- **The held-out tasks were too easy.** Every arm passed 16 of 16 except the different genome (15). The skill-carry gate is met by the frames, but the effect of the skills on performance is unmeasured. The newborn also compiled its own procedures during the held-out session.
- **The world-model result is not genome-specific.** The different genome was also far better calibrated than the newborn, so the result shows that a learned world model carries across models, not that this particular one does.
- **No neural capability moved.** Skills here are procedures, not trained weights. Carrying learned weights or regrowing them on a new model is a later proof.
- **Small and single.** Two local models, one run, small task counts.

## Next experiments

1. **Neural learning without corrupting the mind:** train on one cortex and show the genome's records stay intact and consistent.
2. **Harder held-out tasks**, so that the effect of carried skills can be measured.
3. **Migration of a mature mind** with a longer history across a model upgrade.
